The Gonstead analysis

Our specific and individualized approach.

Using the Gonstead Technique, we take an individualized approach to every practice member through a thorough spinal and nervous system analysis. This allows us to deliver precise care based on objective findings, not guesswork.

  1. 01

    Case history

    Every visit begins with a detailed health history. By understanding your health concerns, lifestyle, and goals, we gain valuable insight into your overall health and nervous system function.

  2. 02

    X-ray analysis

    When clinically appropriate, digital X-rays provide a clear view of your spine, allowing us to analyze and determine the most precise and individualized adjustment. To see is to know and to not see is to guess.

  3. 03

    Instrumentation

    Using a Nervoscope®, we measure subtle temperature differences along the spine that indicate areas of nerve interference. This objective analysis helps us pinpoint where an adjustment is needed.

  4. 04

    Visualization

    We carefully observe your posture, movement, and gait to identify patterns that may indicate spinal imbalance or altered function. These visual findings become an important part of your individualized analysis.

  5. 05

    Palpation

    Through skilled hands-on assessment, we evaluate how your spine moves and locate areas of restricted motion or misalignment. This helps us determine exactly where and how to deliver a precise Gonstead adjustment.

Frequently asked questions

The questions parents ask first.

All 11 questions
Do you see children?

Yes. Chiropractic care can be adapted for patients of all ages, from infants to seniors. Pediatric adjustments use gentle, age-appropriate techniques. Kids are often the ones who need chiropractic the most!

Will I be adjusted on my first visit?

Your chiropractor will first determine whether an adjustment is appropriate and safe based on your examination findings, or if additional examination, study, or referral is necessary.

Is the "popping" sound necessary for an adjustment?

No, the sound sometimes heard during an adjustment is simply gas being released from a joint. A successful adjustment does not depend on hearing a popping sound.
